# SpaceMax grant pitch journey

Working format: approximately five minutes across 11 lean slides. This is a narrative and speaking draft, ready to shape into a deck. The exact grant, scoring rubric, funding amount, timeline, and team credentials were not supplied. Bracketed fields need your actual details.

## The idea to repeat

**SpaceMax is developing custom-fit storage that turns awkward, underused areas into useful, organized space.**

The customer benefit is making the space they already have easier to use and keep tidy. The proposed innovation is the guided process that connects the customer's real space and obstacles to a storage configuration, then to a reviewed physical product.

Use one under-sink example to explain the approach before revealing the broader range. Present an exposed bathroom sink as the recommended first pilot, because it matches the existing demonstrator. The broader product concepts remain part of the vision.

## 1. SpaceMax: storage designed around your space

**Time:** 15 seconds

**On the slide:**

> Custom-fit storage for the awkward spaces we already have.

**Say:** “SpaceMax is developing storage around the space people actually have. We want to turn awkward, underused areas into places that are easier to organize and use every day.”

**Show:** One large existing-space photo beside its proposed storage concept. Label the second image “Design concept.” Keep this same bathroom as the example through the demonstration.

**Bridge:** “The problem becomes clear when you look under this sink.”

## 2. Awkward spaces make staying organized harder

**Time:** 25 seconds

**On the slide:**

> Pipes and awkward dimensions leave useful space difficult to organize.

**Say:** “Most people move into a space they did not design. Under this sink, plumbing interrupts the storage area and supplies gather on the floor. A standard organizer can still leave gaps or fail to accommodate what someone needs to store. Our starting customer wants a tidier space and a solution that fits their daily routine.”

**Show:** The original bathroom photo. Point to the visible plumbing, unused height, and supplies. Keep the explanation specific to what the image actually shows.

**Bridge:** “That makes the actual space the starting point for the design.”

## 3. A configuration built around the actual space

**Time:** 25 seconds

**On the slide:**

> The customer's dimensions and obstacles shape the storage layout.

**Say:** “Our approach starts with a photo, measurements, and the obstacles that the storage needs to work around. Customers also tell us what they want to store. The proposed innovation is a guided configuration process that connects those inputs to a design that can move into fit review and fabrication.”

**Show:** The same photo with the proposed storage area and plumbing boundary marked. Keep annotations sparse.

**Bridge:** “Here is what that experience looks like.”

**Positioning note:** Describe this as your approach and development opportunity. A claim that it is unique, patented, or superior to named competitors would need separate evidence.

## 4. One bathroom, four steps

**Time:** 50 seconds

**On the slide:**

1. Add a photo and describe what needs storing.
2. Enter measurements and mark obstacles.
3. Explore the storage configuration.
4. Choose a layout and save the concept.

**Say:** “The customer starts with their space. They enter dimensions and mark the plumbing area. In our current demonstration, widening that marked area makes the adjacent shelves shorten. They can then compare layouts and save a concept for further review.”

**Show:** A short live demonstration or recording of the actual prototype. Spend the most time on changing the plumbing boundary and showing the shelving respond. End on the selected layout.

**Bridge:** “Those choices connect the design to how someone actually uses the space.”

**Accuracy note:** The current prototype uses manually entered dimensions or prepared example values. Its two-dimensional preview responds to a marked obstacle band. It does not automatically measure a photo or verify a real installation. Keep a brief visible label: “Interactive concept prototype. Fit review required.”

## 5. Storage for the way people use it

**Time:** 20 seconds

**On the slide:**

> A place for everyday essentials, with a choice of what stays visible.

**Say:** “Some customers want open shelves so everything is easy to reach. Others want supplies out of sight, or a combination of both. Our aim is to make better use of the available space, keep it looking clean, and reduce the everyday burden of keeping things organized.”

**Show:** Open, concealed, and combination concepts of the same example. Keep the finish consistent so the layout differences remain clear. A see-through view can help explain the space reserved around plumbing.

**Bridge:** “We can test that value in one focused use case before extending the approach.”

**Accuracy note:** Reduced mental clutter is part of the intended customer benefit. Do not present it as a measured mental-health outcome.

## 6. A focused first product, with room to grow

**Time:** 25 seconds

**On the slide:**

> First pilot: storage around an exposed bathroom sink.
>
> Proposed first customers: households with exposed bathroom sinks who need a dedicated place for everyday supplies.
>
> Wider range: under-sink storage, drawer and cabinet organization, cable organization, custom toilet stools, and storage around the toilet.

**Say:** “We recommend starting with the bathroom sink shown in our demo. That gives us a specific fit problem to test. The wider vision includes other under-sink spaces, drawers and cabinets, cable organization, and products around the toilet. These serve bathrooms, kitchens, living rooms, and bedrooms, with potential applications in commercial spaces later.”

**Show:** Make the first pilot visually dominant. Use a smaller overview for the wider range. Keep commercial examples such as workstations, staff kitchens, restrooms, and guest rooms in the speaking notes or appendix.

**Bridge:** “The first pilot also gives us a clear product to sell and a delivery process to test.”

**Scope note:** Toilet stools are a separate product type with their own design and testing needs. Commercial applications also need their own validation. The unifying idea is designing useful products for the available space.

**Customer evidence to add:** Confirm the actual pilot segment and recruitment area or channel. Include one real finding from conversations or observations that supports the need, with its source and sample size. This evidence is still pending in the supplied materials.

## 7. A clear path from design to product

**Time:** 25 seconds

**On the slide:**

> Proposed model: sell custom storage kits through a guided online design process.
>
> Human fit review and fabrication connect the saved concept to the physical product.

**Say:** “Our proposed starting model is direct product sales. The customer creates a concept online, a reviewer checks critical dimensions and installation needs, and a manufacturing partner produces the kit. The pilot will establish what that process costs and what customers are willing to pay.”

**Show:** A simple sequence of the customer concept, fit review, fabrication, and finished kit. Mark the review and production stages as planned.

**Bridge:** “We already have a way to demonstrate the configuration experience. The next step is to test the physical product and the buying decision.”

**Accuracy note:** This is a recommended business model, not evidence of an existing manufacturing agreement, operational ordering system, or confirmed pricing.

## 8. The prototype and the next questions

**Time:** 25 seconds

**On the slide:**

| Available to demonstrate | Next to validate |
|---|---|
| Guided photo and measurement workflow | Whether customers can provide the required information reliably |
| Responsive storage concept and layout choices | Whether a reviewed design can be fabricated and fitted |
| Saved concept brief | Production cost and willingness to buy at a real price |

**Say:** “We have an interactive concept prototype that demonstrates the customer workflow. Our next questions are practical: can customers provide useful measurements, can we manufacture and install the design, and does the result justify the price for them?”

**Show:** One real prototype screenshot and a short list of the next validation questions. Add actual customer feedback or physical prototype evidence only if available.

**Bridge:** “Those are the questions our grant-funded pilot would answer.”

## 9. What the grant will enable

**Time:** 45 seconds

**On the slide:**

| Proposed milestone | Evidence it should produce |
|---|---|
| Test the customer measurement and configuration journey | Observed completion, common errors, and required support |
| Develop the fabrication and assembly approach | Reviewed drawings, installation sequence, and supplier cost estimates |
| Build and fit physical prototypes | Recorded fit issues, access checks, and design revisions |
| Run a customer pilot at a stated price | Usability feedback, purchasing decisions, and delivery costs |

**Say:** “The funding would help turn the digital concept into a tested physical offering. We would first test how customers provide their information, then develop the fabrication and assembly approach. Physical prototypes would reveal fit and access problems. A customer pilot would then test whether people find the product useful and will buy it at a realistic price.”

**Show:** A milestone timeline with the actual proposed duration and budget once known. Assign a deliverable and cost to each stage.

**Bridge:** “We will assign clear responsibility for each stage.”

**Planning note:** Set target counts and pass criteria before submission, once the grant budget and schedule are known. Potential measures include independent workflow completion, measurement corrections after review, physical fit and assembly results, willingness to pay, and delivered unit cost. These are proposed measures, not results.

## 11. The ask and the result

**Time:** 20 seconds

**On the slide:**

> We are seeking [grant amount] over [time period] to develop and test [defined under-sink pilot].
>
> Deliverables: reviewed physical prototypes, customer pilot findings, and a costed production plan.

**Say:** “We are seeking [amount] to complete this work over [period]. By the end, we aim to have tested physical prototypes, customer evidence, and a costed path to production. SpaceMax starts with one awkward space and a clear ambition: help people make more of the space they already have.”

**Show:** The request, time period, and three concrete deliverables. Pair them with the same bathroom concept from the opening.

## A short spoken explanation

“SpaceMax is developing custom-fit storage for awkward, underused spaces. Think about the area under a bathroom sink: the pipes interrupt the space, supplies gather on the floor, and finding an organizer that fits can be difficult.

Our approach starts with the customer's photo and measurements. They mark the obstacles, explore a storage layout, and choose how they want to use it. Our prototype demonstrates that process, including a shelving sketch that responds when the marked plumbing area changes.

We propose starting with under-sink storage, then extending the approach to other organization products for homes and businesses. Grant funding would help us test the measurement process, build physical prototypes, and run a customer pilot, so we can establish what fits, what it costs, and what people will buy.”

## Keep the good parts without crowding the pitch

Put one message and one main visual on each slide. Use the talk track to connect ideas. Keep detail available in an appendix:

- Full product range and room-by-room residential applications.
- Commercial examples and the additional requirements to investigate.
- Open, concealed, combination, and see-through concept views.
- Measurement confirmation, service access, materials, assembly, and fit review details.
- Detailed pilot design, budget, timeline, supplier estimates, and team credentials.
- Actual customer research, competitor comparison, and supporting evidence as these become available.

The core story should make the customer problem understandable, demonstrate the proposed response, establish a practical route to a physical product, and make the use of grant funding concrete.
