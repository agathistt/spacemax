# SpaceMax: two-presenter grant pitch

## Presenter 1 — Slides 1–3

### Slide 1: Storage designed around your space

*Show the original bathroom photo. Label any proposed after image “Design concept.”*

Think about one spot in your home that never stays organized. Maybe it’s the bottles under your sink, or a drawer where everything ends up piled together.

We’re [Name] and [Name], and we’re developing SpaceMax: custom-fit storage for awkward, underused spaces.

### Slide 2: The problem

Take this bathroom. There’s space under the sink, but the pipes make it difficult to use. Supplies end up on the floor, and a standard organizer might leave gaps or get in the way.

People want their homes to feel tidy. But staying organized is harder when their belongings don’t have a place that works.

### Slide 3: Our approach

SpaceMax starts with the customer’s actual space, its measurements, and the obstacles inside it. We also ask what they need to store.

The idea is to help them explore a storage layout designed around those details.

[Presenter 2’s name], let’s show how that works.

### Slide 5: Why it matters

*Show open, concealed, and combination layouts.*

Customers can choose open shelving for easy access, concealed storage for a cleaner look, or a combination.

Our aim is to give everyday items a useful home, so the space is easier to use and keep tidy, with less clutter to think about.

### Slide 6: Where we start and where we can grow

For our first pilot, we propose focusing on households with exposed bathroom sinks and limited storage.

The wider vision includes drawer and cabinet organization, cable organization, custom toilet stools, and storage around the toilet.

These ideas could serve bathrooms, kitchens, living rooms, and bedrooms, with commercial applications later.

They share one principle: making better use of the space that’s already there.

[Presenter 1’s name] will explain how we plan to turn the design into a product.

## Presenter 1 — Slides 7–9

### Slide 7: How the business would work

Our proposed business model is direct sales of custom storage kits.

Customers would create a concept online. A reviewer would check the dimensions and installation needs, and we would work with a manufacturing partner to produce the kit.

The pilot would establish the production cost and a price customers are willing to pay.

### Slide 8: What we have and what comes next

We have an interactive concept prototype that demonstrates the customer journey.

Next, we need to test how reliably customers can provide the information, whether the designs can be built and fitted, and whether customers find enough value to buy them.

### Slide 9: What the grant will enable

Grant funding would support that work.

First, we would test the measurement process with potential customers. Then we would develop the fabrication approach and build physical prototypes.

Finally, we would run a customer pilot at a real price, tracking usability, fit issues, and delivery costs.

That would give us evidence to improve the product and decide how to bring it to market.

## Rehearsal notes

- Keep the four speaking blocks. Three handoffs are enough to make this feel like one presentation.
- Allow about 20 seconds for demo actions and pauses. Rehearse with the completed team and funding lines to check the actual duration.
- While one presenter speaks, the other advances the slides. During the demo, Presenter 1 operates the controls.
- If using screenshots or a recording, replace “Watch what happens when we widen…” with “This example shows how the shelf layout changes when the marked plumbing area widens.”
- Keep proposed imagery labelled as a design concept. The current demo uses manually entered or prepared measurements, and its preview does not verify physical fit.
- This draft uses the proposed under-sink pilot and product-sales model from the pitch journey. Confirm those choices, add real team details, and match the ask to the actual grant before presenting.
