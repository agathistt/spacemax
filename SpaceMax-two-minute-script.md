# SpaceMax: two-minute pitch for two presenters

Core positioning: SpaceMax uses neuroaffirming interior design to optimize spaces around the sensory preferences, routines, and everyday needs of the people who live in them. Custom-fit storage is the first application of that approach.

Aim for about one minute each, with one handoff. Replace the funding placeholders and rehearse at your natural pace. Use a bathroom concept image as a visual, without a live demo. Research notes below are for the deck and questions, not part of the spoken script.

## Gia

We’re Gia and Kelly, and SpaceMax uses neuroaffirming interior design to optimize spaces around the people who live in them.

That means starting with how each person experiences and uses their environment.

For some people with ADHD or autism, clutter and disorganization can add overwhelm and make everyday tasks harder.

We ask: What makes your routine difficult? What helps you find things? What do you want visible, and what would you prefer tucked away?

Those answers guide the design. One person’s best layout may look very different from another’s.

Our aim is to create spaces that support everyday functioning and wellbeing.

Kelly will explain our first application.

## Kelly

We’re beginning with custom-fit under-sink storage. Our prototype lets customers add a photo, enter measurements, and explore shelving around marked obstacles such as plumbing.

Our proposed service would combine a personal design brief with a custom storage kit, reviewed for fit before manufacturing.

Our approach is research-informed, drawing on work such as Keren MacLennan’s studies of autistic adults’ sensory environments.

We’re seeking [amount] over [time period] to build physical prototypes and run a pilot with neurodivergent users.

We’ll test fit, cost, and whether the designs support participants’ routines and preferences.

Our wider vision is to extend this approach across homes and commercial spaces.

SpaceMax: space optimization, designed around you.

Thank you.

## Research notes for the deck and questions

Use “research-informed neuroaffirming design.” These sources support the rationale for attending to individual needs and environments. They do not establish SpaceMax's effectiveness or constitute endorsements by the authors. Reduced overwhelm and improved wellbeing are intended outcomes to investigate.

### Keren MacLennan and colleagues: sensory environments

Their participatory study, “It Is a Big Spider Web of Things”: Sensory Experiences of Autistic Adults in Public Spaces, used seven focus groups with 24 autistic adults. Participants described factors that made environments more enabling or disabling, including sensory input, space, predictability, adjustments, and opportunities for recovery. The findings support asking people about their own needs. This was qualitative research about public spaces, not a test of storage products.

[Research team's study summary](https://sensorystreet.uk/it-is-a-big-spider-web-of-things-sensory-experiences-of-autistic-adults-in-public-spaces/) · [Paper, 2023 issue](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10726197/)

MacLennan, Sarah O’Brien, and Teresa Tavassoli also studied the sensory experiences of 49 autistic adults in “In Our Own Words” (2022, published online in 2021). Their analysis highlighted control and management of sensory experiences, among other themes.

[In Our Own Words: The Complex Sensory Experiences of Autistic Adults](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34255236/)

### Darby Saxbe and Rena Repetti: home environments and wellbeing

In “No Place Like Home” (2010), they studied home-tour descriptions from 60 spouses in dual-income households. Among the women, descriptions involving clutter and unfinished aspects of the home were associated with flatter daily cortisol slopes and increasing depressed mood over the day. This was a small observational study. It does not show that clutter caused those outcomes or that installing storage improves mental health, and it was not an ADHD or autism study.

[No Place Like Home: Home Tours Correlate With Daily Patterns of Mood and Cortisol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19934011/)

### ADHD: individualized environmental modifications

NICE guidance recommends assessing individual needs when modifying environments to reduce the day-to-day impact of ADHD. Examples include reducing distractions and adjusting lighting or noise. This supports a personalized approach, not a universal requirement for minimalism or a particular storage layout.

[NICE ADHD guidance: environmental modifications](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations#terms-used-in-this-guideline)

The National Autistic Society also identifies visually crowded or cluttered settings as potential sensory triggers for some autistic people and emphasizes that sensory experiences vary.

[National Autistic Society: autism and sensory processing](https://www.autism.org.uk/advice-and-guidance/about-autism/sensory-processing)

## Presentation notes

- Lead with neuroaffirming interior design as the method. Explain space optimization as how well the environment supports its occupant. Present custom storage as the first application.
- The questions about routines and sensory preferences describe the intended design approach. The existing prototype demonstrates physical configuration, not a validated sensory assessment or comprehensive interior-design service.
- Show the wider range visually if needed: drawer and cabinet organizers, cable organization, custom toilet stools, and toilet-area storage. The short speech prioritizes the person, the first product, and the grant-funded pilot.
- Treat open versus concealed storage as an individual preference to explore with users. Do not prescribe either layout based only on a diagnosis.
- The current prototype uses entered or prepared dimensions and a two-dimensional obstacle preview. Physical fit and installation still need review.
- Avoid claiming that the researchers endorse SpaceMax, that the product treats ADHD or autism, or that it has already demonstrated mental-health benefits.
